# Traffic estimation on full graph of transport network using GPS data of bus movements 

Ivan Derevitskiy[1] , Daniil Voloshin[1] , Leonid Mednikov[2] and Vladislav Karbovskii[1 ] 

_1ITMO University, Saint-Petersburg, Russian Federation._

## **Abstract** 

Fast and accurate assessment of the road network loading on the city level is necessary for a number of applications, including operational control in cargo logistics, passenger transportation and work of emergency services. One of the prominent ways to achieve it is to use transport vehicles with geolocation equipment on board to probe traffic conditions. The issue is that the distribution of devices among the vehicles operating in the network is at times sparse and uneven. This creates the necessity to reconstruct actual traffic conditions from incomplete data. In this paper, we address the issue with suggesting the methodology capable of reconstructing traffic conditions for the whole road network from GPS tracks generated by public transportation vehicles. The method has been tested for the dataset collected for the road network of Saint Petersburg. The proposed approach allows to calculate time of a ride between two pairs of addresses located not more than 15 kilometers (in a straight line) apart with the Mean Absolute Percentage Error of just over 20 percent. 

_Keywords:_ Global Positioning System _,_ bus and car relationship, transport modeling, traffic estimation 

## 1 Introduction 

Intensifying automobilization of contemporary metropolises leads to the increased load of their transport infrastructure. For maintaining safe and rapid passage of large numbers of vehicles, a detailed and timely data on the state of the transport network is necessary. 

The data on the transport load of different edges of urban transport network are used by such navigational services as TomTom, Magellan, and Garmin (HubPages 2012) for the calculation of optimal routes. Previously, the distribution of travel times of vehicles has been calculated with the help of the transport network load in the research of (Herring et al. 2010). Alternatively, the load of the transport network has been estimated through different sources of information such as GPS sensors, cameras and radars (Zhang et al. 2013). 

In this paper, we have described the method for calculating city’s transport network load with the help of data from GPS sensors installed on buses in St. Petersburg, Russia. St. Petersburg is a metropolis with the population of around 5 million people. According to (Amosov & Safina 2015), the degree of automobilization the city in 2013 amounted to 295 vehicles per 1000 inhabitants. The complex transport situation of the city is also magnified by the system of draw bridges that isolate some parts of the city at nighttime. Traffic congestions are formed on the routes leading to city center at rush hours (data of road network load statistics from Yandex.Maps service https://yandex.ru/maps).

The distinctive feature of the approach presented herein is the calculation of the load values of those edges of transport networks that were lacking GPS data with the help of load values of the adjacent edges. For the analysis of the correctness of  passage speed calculation in this research we use the approach described in (Zhang et al. 2013). The approach is based on the calculation of the travel time based on the obtained values on transport load and its comparison with the known travel time values. In this research the data on passenger vehicle’s travel time between pairs of different addresses of St. Petersburg is used. The data is obtained from the service Yandex.Maps (https://maps.yandex.com). The service provides information about the shortest route considering the current load of the transport network. 

The structure of this paper looks as follows. The second part describes the existing approaches to the calculation of transport network load by GPS sensors installed on vehicles. The third part describes the research methodology and problem statement. The part 4 describes the methodology of obtaining the load values of the edges of city’s transport network. The fourth part also describes the approach used for the accuracy identification of the obtained values. In the fifth part some results of the performed experiment are described. The part 6 is dedicated to conclusions and thoughts about future researches. 

## 2 Related Works 

GPS as well as radars, cameras and loop detectors is one of the main ways of obtaining data on the traffic situations (Zhang et al. 2013). The papers performed on traffic estimation with the help of GPS sensors, may be classified according to the type of transport network subgraph, the type of transport used and the methods used for obtaining the loading values of a certain segment of the transport network. Moreover, the papers may be classified according to the further use of the obtained data. 

According to the type of subgraph, the papers may be divided into the investigation of a certain route (graph edges sequence of the transport network) and into the investigation of crossed routes sets. In the research of (Zhang et al. 2013) cell phones' GPS sensors are used for traffic estimation on motorway I- 880 in the San Francisco Bay, CA. Check of the obtained data is performed by comparing the travel time (from traffic model) with the actual data on the travel time along the motorway. In (Chen et al. 2007) is described how with the help of GPS sensors installed on the taxi cars in Shanghai the loading of the main traffic network of the city was restored. The distinctive feature of the paper is the weighted algorithm of passage speed calculation on different segments of traffic network.  One of the advantages of a certain route research (as compared to the investigation of transport routes) is the smaller spatial and temporal heterogeneity of GPS data. The disadvantage of the first group research is the smaller range of transport network. However even in the researches of the second group only the main roads of the transport network are covered, while the methods of transport loading calculation which cover the transport network entirely are virtually unexplored. This shows the novelty of our research. 

According to the type of the transport we can classify the papers as follows: those that use the data on passenger vehicle transportation, buses and special transport such as taxi. The data obtained from GPS sensors installed on passenger vehicles in Ann Arbor, Michigan are used in the research (Yoon et al. 2007) to restore the data on transport network segments . In order to calculate the speed values on roads the method of threshold classification was used which is based on the comparison of temporal and spatial distribution of passenger vehicles' speed values. In addition, Yoon studies the influence of cars’ stop time at traffic lights on the travel time. In (Bacon et al. 2011) is described an instrument using GPS data on bus passage for obtaining the values of passage speed on certain segments of traffic network. The values are calculated with the consideration of slowing at the traffic lights and bus stops. 

Due to the differences in technical characteristics of different types of transports, the characteristics of their movement in equal road conditions may differ as well. The distinctive feature of bus usage for calculation of the transport network edges' loading is the necessity to consider the differences between the bus movement and the passenger vehicle movement. In (Kieu et al. 2015), in order to calculate the car speed,  were used the models describing the ratio of cars' average velocity and the buses' average velocity on coinciding areas of transport network. In the models obtained by the author the quantity of traffic lights and bus stops located on the route's area is also taken into account. 

The methods used for obtaining the values of passage speed on certain areas of transport network using GPS data, are based on the usage of speed values of vehicles moving along the certain segment in the course of a certain time interval.  In order to obtain the data on the transport network loading with the help of GPS, in the research (Kong et al. 2013) were used curve-fitting-based method and vehicletracking-based method. First method used adaptive curve model approximating the value of the speed on the road. The second method uses a weighted sum of average speeds. The distinctive feature of this research is the filtration method used for the inaccurate data obtained by GPS sensors. The method is based on the usage of preceding and succeeding sensor tracks. In our research is also used the vehicle tracking- based method for the calculation of the traffic loading in the transport network’s edges. 

The obtained data on the traffic may be used for the optimal route search, for the future transport network loading forecast and for the identification of car accidents. In the research of (De Fabritiis et al. 2008) the values of passage speed of network edges were used for the constructions of 15 and 30 minutes’ prediction models of transport network loading. With the help of macroscopic model of travel time between two points, the researches (Bejan & Gibbens 2011) calculated the map of accessibility for Сambridgeshire Сounty Сouncil on the subgraph of the bus routes included in the graph of transport network in Cambridge, England. The main macroscopic models of travel time calculation performed with the help of the data on transport network edges’ loading are described in the research of (Tsekeris & Skabardonis 2004). 

Google company uses GPS sensors on the users’ mobile devices for the submission of the data on current transport network loading. A fair amount of approaches to the solution of transport tasks that include the usage of transport network loading data, determines the timeliness of the researches dedicated to this problem. The novelty of the approach described in our research consists in the method of transport network load calculation on the whole graph of the transport network which is aimed at making the obtained data more accurate. 

## 3 Problem statement and the methodology 

## 3.1 Problem statement 

This research is based on the following problem statement: GPS sensors are installed on a number of ST. Petersburg buses. Buses run daily on special city’s bus tracks saving their location data. It is possible to calculate velocities of buses with the help of the location change data. According to (Kieu et al. 2012) the cars’ and buses’ speeds in a similar traffic situation on small distances (a couple of km) are comparable. Thus it is possible to calculate the car’s speed when it moves along the city’s transport network areas which are parts of bus routes. But the bus routes run only along some of the traffic network graph edges of the city. Nevertheless, when calculating the travel time of passenger vehicle, it is necessary that the loading of all graph edges be considered. The purpose of this research is to study the efficiency of traffic estimation methods (using traffic data of St.Petersburg as an example). The estimation efficiency checking is performed with the help of data on the travel time of a passenger vehicle over different periods of time obtained with the help of Yandex Maps. 

The majority of researches dedicated to the calculation of transport network’s load using GPS are based on the calculation of the passage speed of a certain edge of transport network graph with the

## 3.2 Data collection methodology 

Data on public transport in the city has been fetched from the “St. Petersburg public transport portal”[*] . It provides users with general information about land transport routes as long as real-time data on vehicle positions with temporal resolution of 10 seconds. Real-time data about one vehicle includes its geographic coordinates, a direction (in angular degrees), and a route identifier. 

Real-time data collection was performed during the period from 10 august 2016, until 12 august 2016 (only weekdays). Only buses were taken into account within this research. During a day the number of buses almost reached 1600. 

It should be mentioned that the information does not contain the identifier particular vehicle. Thus the obtaining of observations belonging to one particular vehicle is another separate task. In this research is considered the obtaining of road graph’s load values within the period from 6am to 12am due to the small number of buses in the affected set for the period from 12am to 6am. 

The data on travel time between the address pairs were obtained by the service Yandex.Maps. On the whole the data sets contain the data on averaged travel time data between 2717 pairs of addresses covering the territory of St. Petersburg. The methodology of choice addresses  was described in detail in the research of (Knyazkov et al. 2015) and is based on city’s population density and on the location of important medical establishments. The number of addresses was selected on the basis of the request limitations on Yandex.Maps service. The data was collected once in every hour within the period from August 10, 2016 until August 12, 2016. 

The data on road network graph of St. Petersburg was obtained using the Open Street Map service. The data of Open Street map is developed by its users. The service contains data on the location of traffic lights and motorways necessary for the calculation of transport network’s load. Moreover, the service contains data on roads which are parts of bus routes. This makes easier the projection procedure on the road graph. 

## 4 The methods restoration of transport’s network load 

## 4.1 Bus route graph’s load restoration 

In the current research 3 methods of St. Petersburg bus graph edges’ load restoration are used. Each method is based on  the Aggregate-Based Method described in the research of (Zhang et al. 2013). 

_Method 1_ . The first approach may be divided into a couple of steps. At the first step all GPS sensors’ tracks are projected on transport graph’s edges. The data on St. Petersburg transport graph is obtained by the Open Street Map service and is described in detail in the chapter about input data. Since the data presented by GPS sensors is not precise, the coordinates of buses projected on the graph are adjusted by the method described in the research of (Kong et al, 2013). The filtration method is based on projection accuracy check done with the help of the previous vehicle’s location. 

The second step consists in selecting observation pairs that belong to the same specific vehicle. This should be done because GPS data does not present any transport identification (more information is presented in the chapter about input data description). Two closest observations found along the network edge are considered to be related to a single vehicle if they: (a) belong to the same bus route; (b) are separated by a 10-second time intervals. 

The third and the fourth steps of the algorithm are based on the native method described in (Kong et al. 2013). The difference of our approach consists in the absence of dependence on speed value. Another distinctive feature is the limitation of our observations by those that are received in 1- hour interval. ## 4.2 The restoration of the load of all edges of the transport network graph 

The method of transport network edges’ load restoration described in the current research is based on the assumption that there is a connection between the load of the adjacent edges of the graph. To restore the entire load of St. Petersburg transport network in this research was used a step by step algorithm. The algorithm may be described by the following sequence of operations:

1. For each time interval, using one of the described above methods, passage speed values of edges of parts of bus routes’ subgraph are generated. 

2. For all edges without passage speed values we find adjacent edges with the values. If we find at least one edge, we average the values of the adjacent edges. The resulting value is assigned to the edge without passing speed values. 

3. In case if the set of edges without values contains at least one element (after step 2) it is necessary to turn to step 2. 

On each iteration for part of edges are calculated passage speed values. As a result of the described algorithm to each of the edges of the graph has been assigned the value of the speed of passage because the graph of the road network is connected. 

## 4.3 The calculation of travel time between address pairs 

The travel time for fixed address pairs is calculated separately for each variant of road graph’s load obtained by one of the three described above methods. The time calculation algorithm looks as follows: 

1. For each address we calculate the closest points on St. Petersburg road graph. 

2. For each time interval between the points we calculate the shortest path on the weighted graph using the Dijkstra’s algorithm (weight - passage speed of edges, according to the data on road graph’s load). 

3. The travel time is calculated by one of the three macroscopic models of travel time. 

Within the boundaries of this research 3 macroscopic models of travel time calculation are used with the help of the passage speed values of road graph’s edges, located on the shortest path between the points. The first model is based on the approach described in the research of (Bejan & Gibbens, 2011). The approach consists in the sum of passage time of each edge of the shortest path. The passage time is calculated as the ratio between the edge’s length and passage speed. 

In the second model the stop time at the traffic light is taken into consideration, adding to the travel time calculated by the first model the product of the number of traffic lights and the average stop time at the traffic lights. The model of the average stop time at the traffic lights is described  in (Tsekeris & Skabardonis, 2004). In this study, the average time of 16.3 seconds is assumed to be (calculation using Tsekeris and Skabardonis model). The third model is based on the division of the day time by the peak and non-peak traffic periods. The influence of these periods  on the travel time of buses is described in the research of (Kieu et al. 2015). Thus, when calculating the stop time at the traffic light in the third model, for some day time intervals (8-10 am and 4-7pm) the additional coefficient is added to the product (0 for non-peak period, 1 for peak hours).

## 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose the methodology capable of reconstructing traffic conditions for the whole road network from GPS tracks generated by public transportation vehicles. The method has been tested for the dataset collected for the road network of Saint Petersburg.

The described traffic estimation algorithm is unique due to the usage of the simplified method of generation of transport network edges’ load not containing GPS observations. 

The most effective approach is based on the theory that the higher meanings received by the processed of GPS tracks characterized the state of traffic more than low meanings. Besides, this approach does not use data of GPS tracks received near bus stops because of the specificity of buses. The most difficult approach allows to calculate time of a ride between two pairs of addresses located not more than 15 kilometers (in a straight line) apart with the Mean Absolute Percentage Error of just over 20 percent. 

When using the approaches described in our research, the accuracy of travel time calculation between the points of the graph decreases if the distance between the points increases.
