\section{Quality of Service Scoring for Public Transit}
\label{sec:foundations:qos}

Public-transit operations are evaluated on multiple independent quality
dimensions rather than on a single composite score. A bus that completes
every scheduled trip but does so consistently ten minutes late is failing
in a different way from one that runs precisely on time but skips one
trip in five; a route whose vehicles stay on the published polyline is
not, for that reason alone, also on schedule. The framework introduced by
Srisakaokul \cite{srisakaokul2023} for the Bangkok bus network captures
this separation explicitly by decomposing service quality into three
independent indicators, each computed from vehicle telemetry recorded by
on-board Global Positioning System (GPS) units.

The first indicator is Quality of Service indicator one (QoS-1), trip
completion. It is the fraction of scheduled trips that started and
reached their terminus within the operating period. The indicator is
computed by matching scheduled trip identifiers against telemetry that
records the trip beginning and ending within bounded geographic windows
of the scheduled start and end stops. A scheduled trip whose telemetry
shows the vehicle never reaching the bounded window of the scheduled end
stop is counted as not completed, irrespective of why the trip was cut
short.

The second indicator is Quality of Service indicator two (QoS-2),
on-path operation. It is the fraction of telemetry pings emitted during
an active trip that lie within a fixed-radius buffer around the
scheduled route polyline. A typical buffer radius is fifty metres. The
indicator measures route adherence: a vehicle that diverts from the
published path to bypass congestion or to handle a deviation accumulates
pings outside the buffer and its QoS-2 score falls accordingly.

The third indicator is Quality of Service indicator three (QoS-3),
on-schedule operation. It is the fraction of stop arrivals that fall
within a fixed time tolerance of the scheduled arrival time at that
stop. The tolerance is a parameter of the framework rather than a fixed
constant; the typical choice in the published evaluation is five
minutes. The indicator measures schedule adherence, which is independent
of both completion and route adherence: a vehicle can complete the trip
on the correct path and still arrive at every intermediate stop late.

Two properties of the framework bear on later design. First, the three
indicators are independent in the sense that a route can score high on
completion while scoring low on adherence, or high on path adherence
while scoring low on schedule adherence, so the indicators are reported
separately rather than averaged into a single number. Second, the
indicators are sensitive to urban-canyon GPS noise, which systematically
lowers absolute QoS-2 scores in dense built environments because pings
drift away from the true vehicle position. The indicator is therefore
most informative when compared across routes operated under similar
conditions rather than against an absolute threshold.
