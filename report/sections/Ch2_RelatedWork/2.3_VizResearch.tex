\section{Research on Transit Visualisation}
\label{sec:related:viz}

The visualisation of operational transit data is a separate body of research from the
analytical methods reviewed in the preceding section. A correct quality-of-service
indicator does not on its own convey to a planner where the network is weak; the
indicator must be presented in a chart that surfaces the relevant comparisons and that
permits the planner to drill into the underlying data. The proposed system draws on two
strands of visualisation research: a thesis on visualisation primitives for spatial and
temporal traffic data, and a treatise on the design principles that govern analytical
charts in general.

\subsection{Visualisation Primitives for Spatial-Temporal Traffic Data}
\label{sec:related:viz:primitives}

Liu \cite{liu2022} surveys and contributes original visualisation primitives for
road-traffic data. Three primitives from the thesis are directly relevant to the present
project. The first is a hierarchical density-by-category primitive built from treemap
variants; the primitive renders a quantitative measure that has been partitioned across a
two-level or three-level categorical hierarchy, with each rectangle's area encoding the
measure and the nesting encoding the hierarchy. The second is a set-intersection matrix
primitive for multi-valued categorical data; the primitive renders the size of each
intersection of a family of overlapping sets as a column in a matrix and is well suited
to visualising routes that simultaneously belong to several archetypes. The third is a
hexagonal-bin map primitive built on a globally consistent hexagonal indexing scheme; the
primitive partitions an urban region into uniform hexagonal cells of a configurable size
and renders a quantitative measure per cell.

The thesis also articulates, in full, the interaction model of Multiple Coordinated Views
(MCV) and gives implementation guidance for brushing-and-linking across coordinated
views. In an MCV interface, several charts are bound to the same underlying data such
that a selection made in one chart is reflected immediately as a highlight in every
other chart, without an explicit navigation step. The model is the natural way to
present a single analytical theme through several complementary chart types. Both the
primitives and the interaction model inform the present project. The primitives are
catalogued in Chapter \ref{chapter:foundations} and instantiated in the visualisation
selection of Chapter \ref{chapter:solution}; the interaction model is the basis of the
interaction design described in Section \ref{sec:solution:interaction}.

\subsection{Visual Storytelling Principles}
\label{sec:related:viz:storytelling}

Knaflic \cite{knaflic2015} codifies four design imperatives for analytical charts, namely
to declutter the chart, to focus the reader's attention on the single quantity that the
chart is meant to convey, to annotate the chart so that the reader can read the
conclusion off the chart itself, and to structure the sequence of charts as a narrative
rather than as an unordered collection. The treatise is written for business
communication, but its principles transfer directly to interactive analytical interfaces:
an analytical view is read in the same way a business chart is read, and the same four
imperatives apply. The present project adopts the four principles as design constraints
on the analytical interface. Section \ref{sec:foundations:storytelling} of this report
develops the principles in technical detail; Section \ref{sec:solution:interaction}
documents how they shape the interaction design of the proposed system.
