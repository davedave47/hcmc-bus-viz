\section{Planned User Evaluation}
\label{sec:future:eval}

The user evaluation closes the Phase Two cycle. The methodology is designed
to measure two outcomes: the ability of stakeholders to answer their
canonical analytical questions using the proposed system, and the discovery
of analytical questions the proposed system can answer but that stakeholders
had not previously articulated. The two outcomes are addressed by two
separate waves of the same study, so that scripted task completion and
unscripted exploration do not interfere with one another.

\subsection{Participants}
\label{sec:future:eval:participants}

Three to four participants per user role are recruited, giving twelve to
sixteen total participants. The four roles correspond to the stakeholder
groups established in Section~\ref{sec:solution:problem}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the passenger, who plans and conducts individual journeys on the
        network;
  \item the driver, whose concern is the single route operated during a
        given shift;
  \item the operating company analyst, who is responsible for fleet
        performance and revenue across the routes operated by a single
        company;
  \item the public-transport authority operator, who oversees network
        performance, anomalies, equity, and capacity at the city level.
\end{itemize}

Participants are recruited through professional contacts and through formal
introductions by the project advisor. Each participant is informed of the
purpose of the study and consents to the recording arrangements before the
first session.

\subsection{Procedure}
\label{sec:future:eval:procedure}

The evaluation runs in two waves. The first wave captures whether
participants can answer the analytical questions associated with their
role under guided conditions. The second wave captures what participants do
when the constraint of a script is removed.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Wave 1 (think-aloud).} A scripted set of analytical tasks,
        one set per role, is performed by each participant while a
        moderator observes and records. Each session is approximately
        ninety minutes. The moderator does not intervene unless the
        participant is stuck for more than one minute; the intervention
        threshold is recorded as part of the session log.
  \item \textbf{Wave 2 (free exploration).} The same participants are given
        the proposed system for two weeks with no script. Weekly check-ins
        record what each participant has explored and what they have
        learnt. The exploration log is analysed at the end of the two
        weeks, after which a short exit interview is conducted with each
        participant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Outcome measures}
\label{sec:future:eval:outcomes}

The outcomes measured are (a) per-task completion rate and median
time-to-answer in Wave 1, computed across the scripted analytical tasks for
each role; (b) the number of unsolicited analytical findings reported per
participant per week in Wave 2, extracted from the weekly check-in
transcripts and reconciled against the exploration log; and (c) qualitative
feedback on the interaction model from the exit interview at the end of
Wave 2, coded against the interaction primitives defined in
Chapter~\ref{chapter:solution}. The three measures together cover scripted
effectiveness, unscripted exploration, and subjective experience.
