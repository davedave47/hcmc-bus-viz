\section{Analytical Themes}
\label{sec:solution:themes}

The proposed system organises its analytical content into six thematic
views. Each theme corresponds to one class of analytical question and is
supported by one cluster of aggregate tables produced by layer 4 of the data
architecture (Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture}). The themes are the
top-level organisational units of the analytical surface; the user
navigates between themes from an overview index, and within each theme
selects a single visualisation that addresses the question of interest.

\subsection{The six themes}
\label{sec:solution:themes:six}

Table~\ref{tab:themes:six} lists the six themes, the analytical question
each one answers, and the primary user role to which each theme is
internally anchored. The primary user role is a design grounding only and
does not appear in the user interface.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{The six analytical themes of the proposed system}
  \label{tab:themes:six}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l X l}
    \hline
    \textbf{Theme} & \textbf{Question answered} & \textbf{Primary role} \\
    \hline
    Demand and Crowding & When and where does ridership concentrate across the network? & Passenger \\
    School-Aware Demand & How do the educational institutions of the city shape demand on the bus network? & Passenger and authority \\
    Spatial and Connectivity & How do routes and stops cover the city, and how does the bus network feed the metro? & Passenger and authority \\
    Financial and Operations & What is the revenue, ridership concentration, payment-channel mix, and promotion impact per route and per company? & Operating company \\
    Network Health and Performance & What is the network-wide reliability, what anomalies occur, and what does the service-day calendar look like? & Public-transport authority \\
    Routes & What does a single route look like in detail, and how do routes compare across the network? & Driver and operational analysts \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The School-Aware Demand theme is the most distinctive component of the
proposed system; it is the theme that no existing tool for Ho Chi Minh City
provides, and it is the theme that connects most directly to the dominant
fare class observed in
Section~\ref{sec:solution:dataset:eda}. The remaining five themes cover
network-wide demand, spatial coverage, financial operations, reliability
and anomalies, and per-route detail respectively, so that every functional
requirement in Section~\ref{sec:solution:problem:functional} is addressed
by at least one theme.

\subsection{Alternatives considered}
\label{sec:solution:themes:alt}

Two alternative organisations of the analytical content were considered and
rejected.

The first alternative groups content by user role rather than by analytical
question: one theme each for passenger, operating company, public-transport
authority, and driver. The alternative was rejected because the most
important analytical questions cross role boundaries. The school-aware
demand question, for example, is asked by both passengers (which route
serves my school?) and by the authority (which corridors carry the largest
student loads?); a role-keyed organisation would either duplicate the
content across two themes or assign it arbitrarily to one of them, with the
other role losing access to the question. A question-keyed organisation
sidesteps the duplication by anchoring each theme to a single question that
all roles can ask.

The second alternative groups content by visualisation primitive: one
theme for maps, one for time series, one for ranked bar charts, and so on.
The alternative was rejected because end users do not formulate questions
about chart types. A primitive-keyed catalogue would force the user to
recognise the chart type that answers their question before they could
locate the relevant theme, which inverts the natural exploration order. A
question-keyed catalogue lets the user start from the question and reach
the chart afterwards.

Table~\ref{tab:themes:alternatives} summarises the comparison.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Alternative organisations of the analytical content}
  \label{tab:themes:alternatives}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l X l}
    \hline
    \textbf{Alternative} & \textbf{Reason for rejection} & \textbf{Status} \\
    \hline
    One theme per user role (four themes) & Cross-role questions duplicated or arbitrarily assigned. & Rejected \\
    One theme per visualisation primitive & Inverts the natural exploration order; user must recognise the chart type before reaching the question. & Rejected \\
    Six themes, one per analytical question & Each theme starts from a question end users naturally ask. & Selected \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The chosen six-theme catalogue elevates the analytical question to the
top-level organisational unit and treats the supporting visualisations as
subordinate to the question. The per-theme visualisation selection that
follows in Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} respects this subordination by
selecting each visualisation to fit the question rather than the reverse.
