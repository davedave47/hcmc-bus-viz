\section{Dataset Overview}
\label{sec:solution:dataset}

The two operational streams, the static master tables, and the
exploratory statistics that constrain the design are characterised
below. The reporting is descriptive; design implications are deferred
to Sections~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} through
\ref{sec:solution:interaction}.

\subsection{Data Sources}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:sources}
\label{sec:solution:fields}

Two operational data streams are available from the public dataset release of
the Centre for Public Transport Management of Ho Chi Minh City
\cite{hcmcgps2025}. The first stream is the GPS vehicle telemetry feed, which
records the position, speed, and heading of each active bus at a sampling
period of approximately five seconds. The second stream is the Automated
Fare Collection (AFC) record feed, which records every boarding event sold
through the on-board fare device, with timestamp, route, vehicle, fare
class, and payment channel.

Five static master tables accompany the two operational streams: the route
table (route identifier, name, operating company, list of served stops in
order), the stop table (stop identifier, geographic position, list of routes
that pass through the stop), the vehicle table (vehicle identifier, plate
number, operating company, capacity class), the company table (company
identifier, name, fleet count), and the promotion table (promotion period,
affected fare classes). The master tables are stable across the calendar
window and are used as conformed dimensions in the warehouse design of
Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture}.

The four data sources describe the bus network at different levels of
granularity. Understanding the grain of each source is the prerequisite for
designing the warehouse and for stating what the analytical surface can and
cannot claim.

\begin{itemize}
\item An AFC record describes one boarding event: a single passenger taps in at a single stop on a single trip at a known time, paying a known fare class through a known payment channel. The AFC stream contains no alighting event. The grain of the stream is one boarding per row.
\item A GPS ping describes one vehicle-location observation: a single vehicle is at a single longitude-latitude pair at a known time, with optional auxiliary fields (door-open state, speed). The grain of the stream is one ping per row, with a typical sampling rate of one ping every few seconds per vehicle.
\item The stop table describes the network's static spatial reference: each row is one stop with a stable identifier, name, and geographic coordinate. The grain is one stop per row.
\item The route table describes the network's service definition: each row is one route with an ordered sequence of stops, scheduled headway, and operating company. The grain is one route per row.
\end{itemize}

These four grains determine what each source can support. AFC can report
boarding magnitude per stop, per trip, per route, per hour, and per fare
class. AFC cannot report passenger journeys end-to-end because no alighting
event is recorded. GPS can report vehicle position and speed at high
temporal resolution. GPS cannot report passenger counts because no fare
event is attached to a ping. The stop and route tables are static and do
not vary by time; they serve as the dimensional reference for both event
streams. Each operational stream and each master table is described
below by its column list and a short statement of its meaning. The schema of the AFC stream is event-shaped: every row is a
single boarding, with foreign keys into the route, trip, station, and
(rarely populated) user dimensions, and a Unix epoch timestamp. The GPS
schema is observation-shaped: every row is a single ping with position,
speed, and door-state telemetry. The master schema is reference-shaped:
each row is a static entity with a stable identifier and descriptive
attributes, joined to the event streams through the route and stop keys.

\subsection{Coverage}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:coverage}

\noindent\textbf{Volume and calendar coverage.} The AFC stream covers the
90-day window from 1 January 2026 to 31 March 2026 in its entirety, with
17{,}883{,}623 boarding events recorded. The GPS stream covers the 50-day
window from 20 March 2025 to 10 May 2025 in the full released catalogue;
the local extraction available to the project at the time of writing
contains the single day 18 March 2025, with approximately 720{,}088 vehicle
pings. The two calendar windows do not overlap, so no event-level join
between the two streams is possible.

\noindent\textbf{Cross-source joinability.} The two event streams (AFC
boardings and GPS pings) share three nominal join keys: route identifier,
vehicle identifier, and time. In principle, an analytical query that needs
both passenger demand and vehicle behaviour can join on a combination of
these keys. In practice, two constraints limit how the join can be
performed. First, the AFC and GPS calendar windows do not overlap, so
event-level joining (one AFC boarding to one GPS ping) is impossible.
Second, the route identifier spaces are not identical between the two
sources: the AFC stream carries 128 distinct route numbers and the GPS
stream carries 163, with 121 route numbers shared between them; 7 routes
are AFC-only (fare data but no telemetry) and 42 are GPS-only. The
consequence is that cross-source analysis is
performed at an aggregate grain that absorbs the temporal mismatch. The
safest such grain is the triple of route identifier, hour of day, and day
type (weekday or weekend). At this grain, an AFC measure (typical
boardings per route per hour) and a GPS measure (typical reliability per
route per hour) can be compared without making a row-level join.
Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} embeds this rule in the
warehouse's aggregate layer.

\label{sec:solution:validity}
\noindent\textbf{Validity boundaries of the analytical surface.} The
analytical surface built on these data sources can support certain claims
and must avoid others. The boundaries are stated explicitly so that the
visualisations in Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} carry only claims the
data supports.

\begin{itemize}
\item Claims about boarding magnitude (per stop, per trip, per route, per hour) are supported by AFC and can be made directly.
\item Claims about passenger journeys (origin to destination) are not supported because alighting events are not recorded. A heuristic alighting model can be inferred from subsequent boardings of the same anonymised user identifier, but the AFC user identifier is populated for only about 0.1\% of records, so any such inference is statistically marginal.
\item Claims about live vehicle occupancy at a given moment are not supported. The closest computable surrogate is cumulative boardings per trip, which is a proxy for crowding rather than a count of passengers on the vehicle.
\item Claims about vehicle reliability (trip completion, on-path operation, on-schedule arrival) are supported by GPS and the schedule table, with the urban-canyon caveat noted in Section~\ref{sec:foundations:qos}.
\item Claims that combine demand (AFC) and operations (GPS) at a finer grain than route by hour by day-type are not supported, because the two calendar windows do not overlap and event-level joining is impossible.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} adopts the convention that any chart making
a claim that lies outside these boundaries must explicitly label the claim
as a proxy or estimate.

\subsection{Exploratory Findings}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:eda}

Exploratory analysis of the AFC stream characterises the dominant
distributions of the demand side. Six headline findings frame what
follows; Figure~\ref{fig:eda:headlines} surfaces them at a glance.
Five views of the 90-day window are then presented in detail: the
daily ridership trajectory, the hourly demand by day type, the
fare-class composition over the day, the heavy-tailed distribution of
boardings across routes, and the payment-channel trend.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{images/diagrams/eda_headlines.png}
\caption{Six headline findings from the 90-day AFC exploratory analysis.}
\label{fig:eda:headlines}
\end{figure}

\noindent\textbf{Daily ridership over the 90-day window.} The daily
boarding total traces the overall demand trajectory across the AFC
window. A mid-February trough is visible that aligns with the
Tết public holiday, when much of the city's working and student
population leaves for family travel and the bus network's regular commuter
load drops correspondingly.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eda_daily_boardings.png}
\caption{Daily AFC boardings, 1 January to 31 March 2026}
\label{fig:eda:daily}
\end{figure}

Daily counts oscillate around a weekly cycle for the first three weeks
of January at a median of approximately 228{,}000 boardings per day, then
decline ahead of the Tết public holiday and reach a single-day
minimum on 16 February at 33{,}176 boardings (an 84 percent drop from
the January baseline). Ridership recovers progressively through the
second half of February and returns to the January baseline by the
first week of March.

\noindent\textbf{Hourly demand by day type.} Disaggregating the daily
totals by hour and day type reveals the commuter signature of the
network's weekday demand and the contrasting flatter shape of weekend
demand.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{eda_hourly_by_daytype.png}
\caption{Average hourly boardings, weekday vs weekend}
\label{fig:eda:hourly}
\end{figure}

Weekday demand has two roughly equal peaks (about 22{,}000 events per hour
at approximately 6 AM and 4 to 5 PM); weekend demand is flatter, lower in
absolute magnitude, and shifted to later in the day with no comparable
morning peak.

\noindent\textbf{Fare-class composition by hour.} Decomposing the hourly
totals by fare class shows which sub-population drives each part of the
day.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{eda_fareclass_by_hour.png}
\caption{Hourly boarding composition by fare-class group}
\label{fig:eda:fareclass}
\end{figure}

Across the day, regular cash tickets dominate by volume
(approximately 43 percent of all boardings) but the student fare group
(approximately 36 percent, the HS-SV discounted class) drives a
disproportionate share of the morning peak. Free or subsidised tickets
contribute approximately 16 percent and are roughly flat across the
day; the small remainder is split between paper and other ticket types
(approximately 6 percent combined).

\noindent\textbf{Route volume distribution.} The boarding count per route
is heavy-tailed, with a small number of routes carrying a disproportionate
share of the total. Figure~\ref{fig:eda:pareto} ranks the top 50 routes
and overlays the cumulative share.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eda_route_pareto.png}
\caption{Top 50 routes by boarding volume, with cumulative share}
\label{fig:eda:pareto}
\end{figure}

The boarding distribution is heavy-tailed. Cumulative share reaches 80
percent at the 55th-ranked route of 128 active routes; the top 10 routes
account for roughly 32 percent of all boardings. The pattern motivates the
top-$k$ ranked-bar visualisations chosen in
Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} and the operator-scorecard view in the
Financial and Operations theme.

\noindent\textbf{Payment-channel trend.} Stacking the daily volume by
payment channel reveals whether the cash dominance reported in earlier
statistics is uniform across the window or whether it shifts over time.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eda_payment_trend.png}
\caption{Daily boardings stacked by payment channel}
\label{fig:eda:payment}
\end{figure}

Cash payment dominates throughout the window (approximately 96.2 percent
of daily volume), with a small and roughly constant non-cash band
underneath. The Tết-period dip is visible on both channels in
proportion, confirming that the dip is a network-wide phenomenon rather
than a channel-specific effect.

\noindent\textbf{Anchor institutions for the student-fare flow.} The
student-fare share visible in Figure~\ref{fig:eda:fareclass} is anchored
at eight large educational institutions in the city: the Vietnam National
University System, Ho Chi Minh City University of Technology, the
University of Economics, the University of Transport, the University of
Social Sciences and Humanities, the University of Technology and
Education, the University of Information Technology, and Saigon
University. These eight institutions together correspond to the largest
student-fare flows in the AFC stream and motivate the dedicated
School-Aware Demand theme described in Section~\ref{sec:solution:themes}.

On the operational side, the local one-day GPS extraction for 18 March
2025 records pings from 131 unique vehicles, distributed across nine
analysis routes (29, 41, 57, 61, 84, 99, 102, 141, and 151). The
catalogued full 50-day window covers a broader set of routes; replacing
the local extraction with the full window is part of the phase 2 work.

\subsection{Data Quality}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:quality}

Eleven data-quality observations affect downstream analysis; the full
inventory is given in Table~\ref{tab:dataset:quality} of
Appendix~\ref{appendix:ddl}. Two observations are consequential enough
to shape the architecture of the rest of the chapter. The
calendar-window mismatch rules out any event-level join between the
operational and demand sources and forces every cross-source analysis
to operate at the aggregate level of route by hour of day by day type.
The absence of alighting events rules out any live-occupancy view and
forces the demand-side analytical model to treat the boarding event as
the unit of measurement throughout. The architecture of
Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} addresses both observations
directly: aggregate-grain reconciliation in the conformed layer answers
the first, and a boarding-centric measurement model in the fact and
aggregate layers answers the second.

\subsection{Implications}
\label{sec:solution:eda_to_design}

Five exploratory findings have direct, traceable effects on the design
proposed in this chapter. Each implication points back to the field-level
tables of Section~\ref{sec:solution:fields} or the figures of
Section~\ref{sec:solution:dataset:eda}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The concentration of student-fare tickets into the morning weekday peak (visible in Figure~\ref{fig:eda:fareclass}, with the \texttt{ticket\_type} field of Table~\ref{tab:fields:afc} carrying the fare-class label) is the empirical basis for the School-Aware Demand analytical theme of Section~\ref{sec:solution:themes}. Without the finding, the school-aware theme would have been one of many; with it, the theme becomes the most distinctive contribution of the proposed system relative to the existing tools surveyed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:related}.
\item The cash share of payments evident in Figure~\ref{fig:eda:payment} (approximately 96.2\% across the full window) justifies a payment-adoption view to track how the share evolves over time, but limits the analytical depth available for digital-payment study; Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} reflects this by allocating one chart to payment adoption rather than a separate theme.
\item The heavy-tailed distribution of boardings across routes (Figure~\ref{fig:eda:pareto}, with the \texttt{route\_no} field of Table~\ref{tab:fields:masters} carrying the canonical key) justifies the use of Pareto-style ranked-bar primitives for route comparison in the Demand and Crowding and Financial and Operations themes.
\item The non-overlapping calendar windows of the GPS and AFC streams (the \texttt{datetime} field of Table~\ref{tab:fields:gps} and the \texttt{time\_created\_ticket} field of Table~\ref{tab:fields:afc}) force cross-source analysis to the aggregate grain of route by hour by day type. The warehouse architecture of Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} embeds this rule by materialising the aggregate-by-route-hour table as the only cross-source data product.
\item The visible mid-February trough in daily AFC ridership around Tết (Figure~\ref{fig:eda:daily}) is a natural-experiment window for the anomaly view in the Network Health and Performance theme. The day-type dimension is tagged at warehouse build time so that anomaly visualisations can filter on the window without ad-hoc computation.
\end{enumerate}
