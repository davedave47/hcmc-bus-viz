\section{Dataset Overview and Exploratory Analysis}
\label{sec:solution:dataset}

This section describes the data sources available to the project, reports the
volume and calendar coverage of each source, and presents the basic
exploratory statistics that ground the analytical design of the rest of the
chapter. The reporting in this section is descriptive; design implications
are drawn in Sections~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} through
\ref{sec:solution:interaction}.

\subsection{Data Sources}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:sources}

Two operational data streams are available from the public dataset release of
the Centre for Public Transport Management of Ho Chi Minh City
\cite{hcmcgps2025}. The first stream is the GPS vehicle telemetry feed, which
records the position, speed, and heading of each active bus at a sampling
period of approximately five seconds. The second stream is the AFC fare
record feed, which records every boarding event sold through the on-board
fare device, with timestamp, route, vehicle, fare class, and payment channel.

Five static master tables accompany the two operational streams: the route
table (route identifier, name, operating company, list of served stops in
order), the stop table (stop identifier, geographic position, list of routes
that pass through the stop), the vehicle table (vehicle identifier, plate
number, operating company, capacity class), the company table (company
identifier, name, fleet count), and the promotion table (promotion period,
affected fare classes). The master tables are stable across the calendar
window and are used as conformed dimensions in the warehouse design of
Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture}.

\subsection{Data Model and Analytical Grain}
\label{sec:solution:datamodel}

The four data sources describe the bus network at different levels of granularity. Understanding the grain of each source is the prerequisite for designing the warehouse and for stating what the analytical surface can and cannot claim.

\begin{itemize}
\item An AFC record describes one boarding event: a single passenger taps in at a single stop on a single trip at a known time, paying a known fare class through a known payment channel. The AFC stream contains no alighting event. The grain of the stream is one boarding per row.
\item A GPS ping describes one vehicle-location observation: a single vehicle is at a single longitude-latitude pair at a known time, with optional auxiliary fields (door-open state, speed). The grain of the stream is one ping per row, with a typical sampling rate of one ping every few seconds per vehicle.
\item The stop table describes the network's static spatial reference: each row is one stop with a stable identifier, name, and geographic coordinate. The grain is one stop per row.
\item The route table describes the network's service definition: each row is one route with an ordered sequence of stops, scheduled headway, and operating company. The grain is one route per row.
\end{itemize}

These four grains determine what each source can support. AFC can report boarding magnitude per stop, per trip, per route, per hour, and per fare class. AFC cannot report passenger journeys end-to-end because no alighting event is recorded. GPS can report vehicle position and speed at high temporal resolution. GPS cannot report passenger counts because no fare event is attached to a ping. The stop and route tables are static and do not vary by time; they serve as the dimensional reference for both event streams.

\subsection{Cross-Source Joinability}
\label{sec:solution:joinability}

The two event streams (AFC boardings and GPS pings) share three nominal join keys: route identifier, vehicle identifier, and time. In principle, an analytical query that needs both passenger demand and vehicle behaviour can join on a combination of these keys. In practice, two constraints limit how the join can be performed.

First, the AFC and GPS calendar windows do not overlap. The AFC stream covers 1 January to 31 March 2026 inclusive. The GPS stream covers 20 March to 10 May 2025 inclusive. No calendar day is common to both. Event-level joining (one AFC boarding to one GPS ping) is therefore impossible.

Second, the route identifier spaces are not identical between the two sources. Of the 163 routes present in the AFC stream, 121 also appear in the GPS stream. The remaining 42 routes are AFC-only and have no corresponding telemetry.

The consequence is that cross-source analysis is performed at an aggregate grain that absorbs the temporal mismatch. The safest such grain is the triple of route identifier, hour of day, and day type (weekday or weekend). At this grain, an AFC measure (typical boardings per route per hour) and a GPS measure (typical reliability per route per hour) can be compared without making a row-level join. Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} embeds this rule in the warehouse's aggregate layer.

\subsection{Derived Features}
\label{sec:solution:derived}

Several analytical features are not present in the raw streams but are derived during preprocessing. Each is named here so subsequent sections can refer to it without re-explaining.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hour of day}: the integer 0 to 23 extracted from a boarding timestamp.
\item \textbf{Day type}: a categorical label (weekday, weekend, public holiday) derived from the calendar date and a known table of holidays.
\item \textbf{Fare class grouping}: a small set of coarse categories derived from the more numerous raw fare class codes, for example \{student, adult, elderly, free pass, other\}.
\item \textbf{Payment channel grouping}: a binary grouping of payment channels into cash and non-cash, for views that need to compare digital adoption.
\item \textbf{Route-level demand}: an aggregate per route of the boarding count over a chosen time window.
\item \textbf{Trip-level operational indicators}: per-trip quality of service scores computed from GPS pings using the framework of Section~\ref{sec:foundations:qos}.
\item \textbf{Boardings per trip}: a proxy for crowding computed by attributing AFC boardings to the trip that the boarding belongs to. The proxy is not a direct count of passengers on the vehicle at any moment because no alighting event is recorded.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validity Boundaries of the Analytical Surface}
\label{sec:solution:validity}

The analytical surface built on these data sources can support certain claims and must avoid others. The boundaries are stated explicitly so that the visualisations in Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} carry only claims the data supports.

\begin{itemize}
\item Claims about boarding magnitude (per stop, per trip, per route, per hour) are supported by AFC and can be made directly.
\item Claims about passenger journeys (origin to destination) are not supported because alighting events are not recorded. A heuristic alighting model can be inferred from subsequent boardings of the same anonymised user identifier, but the AFC user identifier is populated for only about 0.1\% of records, so any such inference is statistically marginal.
\item Claims about live vehicle occupancy at a given moment are not supported. The closest computable surrogate is cumulative boardings per trip, which is a proxy for crowding rather than a count of passengers on the vehicle.
\item Claims about vehicle reliability (trip completion, on-path operation, on-schedule arrival) are supported by GPS and the schedule table, with the urban-canyon caveat noted in Section~\ref{sec:foundations:qos}.
\item Claims that combine demand (AFC) and operations (GPS) at a finer grain than route by hour by day-type are not supported, because the two calendar windows do not overlap and event-level joining is impossible.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} adopts the convention that any chart making a claim that lies outside these boundaries must explicitly label the claim as a proxy or estimate.

\subsection{Volume and Calendar Coverage}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:volume}

The AFC stream covers the 90-day window from 1 January 2026 to 31 March 2026
in its entirety, with 17{,}883{,}623 boarding events recorded. The GPS stream
covers the 50-day window from 20 March 2025 to 10 May 2025 in the full
released catalogue; the local extraction available to the project at the time

720{,}088 vehicle pings. The two calendar windows do not overlap, so no

\subsubsection{Observed Patterns in the Exploratory Statistics}

Four patterns recur across the statistics reported above and are noted here
as observation, not design decision. The architectural responses appear
later in this chapter. First, the hourly event distribution exhibits two
peaks of comparable magnitude near the start of the working morning and the
end of the working afternoon, with a midday trough roughly half the peak
magnitude; the shape is consistent with a commuter-dominated ridership and
is not consistent with a tourist or leisure profile. Second, the fare-class
breakdown is concentrated on three large classes (standard adult, student,
and free pass), which together account for above 95\% of all events, with a
long tail of minor classes; any analytical view that does not separate the
student class from the standard adult class will mask the dominant
sub-population. Third, the payment-channel distribution is highly skewed
toward cash, which holds above 96\% of events across the full window; the
skew constrains any analytical question about digital-channel adoption to
a small-fraction analysis with limited statistical resolution at fine
temporal granularity. Fourth, the route-level boarding distribution is
heavy-tailed: ten routes carry roughly one third of all events while the
remaining 153 routes form a long tail, with no single tail route exceeding
1\% individually; analytical comparisons across routes must therefore use
either a logarithmic axis or a top-N restriction to remain readable.

\subsection{Data Quality Observations}
\label{sec:solution:dataset:quality}

Six data-quality observations affect downstream analysis. They are reported
here without design implications; the architectural responses are described
in Section~\ref{sec:solution:preprocessing} and
Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture}. The observations are split across
two tables to keep each table compact.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Data quality observations: temporal and content gaps}
  \label{tab:dataset:quality1}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l X}
    \hline
    \textbf{Observation} & \textbf{Impact on downstream analysis} \\
    \hline
    Calendar-window mismatch between the GPS and AFC streams. & No event-level join is possible; all cross-source aggregation must operate at the level of route by hour of day by day type. \\
    Absence of alighting events. AFC records boardings only. & Live occupancy at a given time is not computable; only cumulative boardings and a boardings-per-trip proxy are available. \\
    The user identifier field in AFC is populated for only 0.1\% of fare records. & Individual-passenger features (loyalty, churn, journey patterns) are unsupported; demand analysis operates strictly at the aggregate level. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Data quality observations: encoding and noise}
  \label{tab:dataset:quality2}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l X}
    \hline
    \textbf{Observation} & \textbf{Impact on downstream analysis} \\
    \hline
    Approximately 91{,}000 schedule rows carry the value \texttt{unknown} for the service-type field. & Schedule-adherence indicators must classify or exclude these rows before any QoS-3 metric can be computed. \\
    GPS positioning noise is systematically higher in dense urban areas, reducing the on-path scoring rate. & QoS-2 scoring requires geographic coordinate rounding before trajectory matching against the route geometry. \\
    The cash field in the AFC raw schema misleadingly includes a small fraction of smartcard deductions. & Preprocessing renames the field to a more precise label and isolates the smartcard deductions into the smartcard channel before any payment-channel breakdown. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Two of the six observations are consequential enough to shape the
architecture of the rest of the chapter. The calendar-window mismatch
between the GPS and AFC streams (the first row of
Table~\ref{tab:dataset:quality1}) rules out any event-level join between
the operational and demand sources, and forces every cross-source analysis
to operate at the aggregate level of route by hour of day by day type. The
absence of alighting events (the second row of the same table) rules out
any live-occupancy view, and forces the demand-side analytical model to
treat the boarding event as the unit of measurement throughout. The
architecture of Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} addresses both
observations directly: aggregate-grain reconciliation in the conformed
layer answers the first, and a boarding-centric measurement model in the
fact and aggregate layers answers the second.

\subsection{From Exploratory Findings to Design Decisions}
\label{sec:solution:eda_to_design}

Five exploratory findings have direct, traceable effects on the design proposed in this chapter:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The concentration of student-fare tickets into morning weekday peaks is the empirical basis for the school-aware analytical theme of Section~\ref{sec:solution:themes}. Without the finding, the school-aware theme would have been one of many; with it, the theme becomes the most distinctive contribution of the proposed system relative to the existing tools surveyed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:related}.
\item The 96\% cash share of payments justifies a payment-adoption view (to track how the share evolves over time) but limits the analytical depth available for digital-payment study; Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} reflects this by allocating one chart to payment adoption rather than a separate theme.
\item The heavy-tailed distribution of boardings across routes (a small number of routes carrying a disproportionate share of total volume) justifies the use of Pareto-style ranked-bar primitives for route comparison in the Demand and Crowding and Financial and Operations themes.
\item The non-overlapping calendar windows of the GPS and AFC streams forces cross-source analysis to the aggregate grain of route by hour by day type. The warehouse architecture of Section~\ref{sec:solution:architecture} embeds this rule by materialising the aggregate-by-route-hour table as the only cross-source data product.
\item The visible nine-day flatline in daily AFC ridership around T\d ết is a natural-experiment window for the anomaly view in the Network Health and Performance theme. The day-type dimension is tagged at warehouse build time so that anomaly visualisations can filter on the window without ad-hoc computation.
\end{enumerate}
