\section{Data Architecture}
\label{sec:solution:architecture}

The design problem addressed by the data architecture is the balance between
interactive query latency on the analytical surface and the build-time cost
of preparing the data behind that surface. Given the fixed analytical
catalogue produced in Section~\ref{sec:solution:themes} and the latency
target stated in requirement N1 of
Section~\ref{sec:solution:problem:nonfunctional}, the architecture must
guarantee bounded query cost for every chart in the catalogue while keeping
the warehouse small enough to satisfy the on-disk size limit of requirement
N4. This section evaluates three candidate architectures against these
constraints and selects one.

\subsection{Candidate Architectures}
\label{sec:solution:architecture:candidates}

The classical three-layer medallion architecture
\cite{databricksMedallion} arranges the data in a raw layer, a conformed
layer of cleaned dimensions and facts, and a presentation layer in which
aggregation is performed on demand at query time. The strength of the
medallion is its support for open-ended ad-hoc analytical query: every
question against the conformed layer is answerable, at the cost of a fresh
aggregation each time. The weakness of the medallion in the present context
is that the on-demand aggregation step cannot meet the warm-render latency
target for an interactive analytical surface that issues hundreds of small
queries per user session.

A second candidate is a star-schema design placed directly over the raw
views produced by preprocessing, without a conformed dimensional layer. The
strength of this candidate is the minimal materialisation cost: only the
fact tables and the dimension tables would need to be created. The weakness
is that the route-identifier reconciliation between the GPS stream and the
AFC stream must be repeated in every analytical query, with the risk that
two visualisations referring to ``route 152'' refer to slightly different
subsets of vehicles or trips if the reconciliation logic drifts.

A third candidate, and the one selected, is a four-layer specialisation in
which the conformed and aggregated layers are split apart. Layer 1 contains
the raw views over the preprocessed data, layer 2 the conformed dimensions,
layer 3 the fact views shaped through the conformed dimensions, and layer 4
the pre-computed aggregate tables that the analytical surface reads from.
The strength of this candidate is the combination of a single source of
truth for the conformed identifiers (in layer 2) and bounded query cost (in
layer 4). The weakness is that the build-time cost is higher than the other
two alternatives, since the aggregate tables must be re-materialised
whenever the underlying data changes.

\subsection{Selected Architecture}
\label{sec:solution:architecture:chosen}

The proposed system adopts the four-layer specialisation. The selection
follows directly from the comparison above: only the four-layer design
combines a guaranteed warm-render latency at the analytical surface with a
single, centrally-defined reconciliation of route identifiers between the
two operational data streams. The higher build-time cost is acceptable
because the build is run rarely (once per data refresh) and because the
preprocessed window is bounded (90 days of AFC and 50 days of GPS at the
upper limit).

Table~\ref{tab:arch:layers} summarises the four layers, their contents,
and their materialisation mode.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Four-layer data architecture: contents and materialisation}
  \label{tab:arch:layers}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l X l}
    \hline
    \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Contents} & \textbf{Materialisation} \\
    \hline
    Layer 1, raw views & Approximately twenty-four columnar views over the preprocessed data, registered automatically when the analytical engine opens the warehouse file. & View (non-materialised). \\
    Layer 2, conformed dimensions & Ten dimension tables: calendar, hour, route, stop, school, company, fare class, payment channel, vehicle, promotion. Reconciles route identifiers across GPS and AFC; pre-tags Tết and parade-week windows. & Persistent table on disk. \\
    Layer 3, fact views & Three fact views: boarding events, vehicle trip records, stop visits. Each fact view is re-shaped through the conformed dimensions of layer 2. & View (non-materialised). \\
    Layer 4, aggregate tables & Eight aggregate tables, each pre-computed for one or more of the analytical themes catalogued in Section~\ref{sec:solution:themes}. The analytical surface reads exclusively from this layer. & Persistent table on disk. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The aggregate layer is the analytical-surface contract. Every chart in the
catalogue of Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} reads from a single aggregate
table; no chart issues a join across multiple aggregates at query time. The
contract is the mechanism that guarantees the warm-render target of
requirement N1.

\subsection{Materialisation and refresh policy}
\label{sec:solution:architecture:refresh}

The conformed dimensions of layer 2 and the aggregate tables of layer 4 are
persistent tables on the on-disk warehouse file. They are re-created in a
single deterministic pass when the upstream preprocessed data changes; the
pass is the warehouse build referenced in requirement N3. The fact views
of layer 3 are not materialised, because the columnar storage of the
underlying preprocessed data already supports the scan throughput required
by the aggregate build at layer 4. The aggregate build at layer 4 therefore
reads from layer 1 through the lightweight fact views of layer 3 and writes
once to the persistent aggregate tables. The total on-disk size of the
warehouse file remains below the limit set in requirement N4 because the
aggregate layer compresses the 17.88-million-row AFC stream into a few
hundred thousand aggregate rows.

\subsection{Refresh and consistency guarantees}
\label{sec:solution:architecture:guarantees}

The warehouse build is idempotent: re-running the build on the same raw
input produces byte-identical conformed-dimension tables and
byte-identical aggregate tables, which satisfies requirement N3. The build
ordering respects the layer dependency: conformed dimensions are rebuilt
before any aggregate that depends on them, and aggregates are written only
after every dimension on which they depend has been written successfully.
At startup, the analytical surface validates the presence of all eight
aggregate tables and refuses to render any theme whose backing aggregate
is missing or whose modification timestamp is older than the most recent
upstream change; the missing or stale theme is reported to the user with a
short diagnostic note rather than rendered against incomplete data.
