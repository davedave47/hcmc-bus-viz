\section{Predictive Extensions}
\label{sec:future:predictive}

Two predictive extensions follow naturally from the historical
foundation laid in Phase~1 and the implementation in Phase~2.
Both consume the existing Layer~4 aggregate tables and require
no revisiting of Layer~1, 2, or~3 of the warehouse.

\paragraph{Real-time arrival-time prediction.}
Lin~\&~Zeng~\cite{linzeng2014} demonstrate a link-node route
representation that projects GPS pings onto a one-dimensional
route path, constructs time--distance trajectories, and
predicts arrival time at downstream stops with the dwell at
time-check stops as the dominant predictor. The same
construction applies to HCMC's fifty-day GPS dataset: the
link-node representation falls out of the existing
\texttt{gps\_stop\_times} view, and the dwell distribution is
already aggregated in \texttt{fact\_stop\_visits.dwell\_seconds}.
The extension lifts Chart~6.5 (the Marey diagram) from a
backward-looking visualisation to a predictive one, with
arrival-time bands rendered on the Detail-mode Routes page.

\paragraph{Anomaly detection on hourly demand.}
The Anomaly Lab of Chart~5.5 currently flags anomalous days at
the network level using a threshold-$T$ slider over fixed
quantile bands. The natural extension is to flag anomalies at
the per-route per-hour grain using statistical-control limits
derived from the ninety-day AFC history (control charts at
$\pm 3\sigma$ on the \texttt{agg\_route\_hour} table). The
extension feeds back into the Network Health card without
requiring a new aggregate.

\paragraph{Bus-as-traffic-sensor map.}
A third, more speculative extension is the bus-as-traffic-sensor
map of
Derevitskiy~\textit{et~al.}~\cite{derevitskiy2016}: aggregating
GPS-derived edge speeds into a road-graph-wide speed estimate
that covers edges where no buses run, via macroscopic
travel-time models. The extension is feasible against the full
fifty-day GPS dataset but is currently outside the dashboard's
scope because the resulting map would require its own card and
its own data contract; it is therefore WON'T-tier for Phase~2
and a candidate for a Phase~3 if the project continues.
