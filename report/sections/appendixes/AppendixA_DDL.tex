\chapter*{Appendix A: Data Warehouse Definitions}
\label{appendix:ddl}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Appendix A: Data Warehouse Definitions}

This appendix documents the data-definition language (DDL) for four key tables of the proposed warehouse. The excerpts are the design-time specifications; the complete warehouse build is Phase Two work scheduled in Chapter~\ref{chapter:conclusion}.

\section*{A.1 Route Dimension Table}

The route dimension table is the canonical descriptor of every route in the network. It conforms the AFC-side route identifier with the GPS-side route number so that downstream facts and aggregates can join on a single key.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, basicstyle=\scriptsize\ttfamily, caption={Route dimension table.}]
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE dim_route AS
SELECT
    a.route_no                       AS route_id,
    a.route_name                     AS route_name,
    a.company_id                     AS company_id,
    a.direction_count                AS direction_count,
    a.stop_count                     AS stop_count,
    a.distance_km                    AS distance_km,
    a.scheduled_trips_per_day        AS scheduled_trips_per_day,
    g.route_no                       AS gps_route_no,
    (g.route_no IS NOT NULL)         AS has_gps_data,
    CAST(NULL AS VARCHAR)            AS archetype
FROM raw_afc_routes        AS a
LEFT JOIN raw_gps_routes   AS g
       ON a.route_no = g.route_no;
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent This dimension is the canonical join surface for routes across all analytical themes.

\section*{A.2 Calendar Dimension Table}

The calendar dimension table covers the AFC analysis window and pre-tags special periods so that downstream queries can filter on T\^et or weekend day-type without re-computing date logic.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, basicstyle=\scriptsize\ttfamily, caption={Calendar dimension table.}]
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE dim_date AS
WITH calendar AS (
    SELECT UNNEST(generate_series(
        '2026-01-01'::DATE,
        '2026-03-31'::DATE,
        INTERVAL 1 DAY
    )) AS date
)
SELECT
    date                                          AS date_key,
    EXTRACT(year  FROM date)                      AS year,
    EXTRACT(month FROM date)                      AS month,
    EXTRACT(week  FROM date)                      AS week,
    EXTRACT(dow   FROM date)                      AS dow,
    (EXTRACT(dow FROM date) IN (0, 6))            AS is_weekend,
    (date BETWEEN '2026-02-14' AND '2026-02-22')  AS is_tet,
    CASE
        WHEN date BETWEEN '2026-02-14' AND '2026-02-22' THEN 'tet'
        WHEN EXTRACT(dow FROM date) IN (0, 6)           THEN 'weekend'
        ELSE                                                 'weekday'
    END                                           AS day_type
FROM calendar;
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent This dimension provides the day-type partitioning used by every aggregate table that groups by day.

\section*{A.3 Boarding-Event Fact View}

The boarding-event fact view joins the raw ticket and payment tables and exposes a flat boarding-event row with foreign keys into the dimension layer.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, basicstyle=\scriptsize\ttfamily, caption={Boarding-event fact view.}]
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW fact_boardings AS
SELECT
    t.ticket_id                       AS ticket_id,
    CAST(t.boarding_time AS DATE)     AS date_key,
    EXTRACT(hour FROM t.boarding_time) AS hour,
    t.route_no                        AS route_id,
    t.stop_id                         AS stop_id,
    t.fare_class                      AS fare_class_id,
    p.channel                         AS payment_channel_id,
    t.vehicle_id                      AS vehicle_id,
    COALESCE(p.amount, 0)             AS amount,
    (COALESCE(p.amount, 0) = 0)       AS is_free_ticket
FROM raw_tickets   AS t
LEFT JOIN raw_payments AS p
       ON t.ticket_id = p.ticket_id;
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent This view is the single boarding-event grain from which every demand and financial aggregate is derived.

\section*{A.4 Route-by-Hour Aggregate Table}

The route-by-hour aggregate is the principal chart-ready table for the demand and crowding theme. It pre-computes boarding counts, student-fare counts, unique-ticket counts, and revenue per route per hour per day-type, so that the analytical surface answers per-route hourly queries in a single point lookup.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, basicstyle=\scriptsize\ttfamily, caption={Route-by-hour aggregate table.}]
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE agg_route_hour AS
SELECT
    f.route_id                                              AS route_id,
    f.hour                                                  AS hour,
    d.day_type                                              AS day_type,
    COUNT(*)                                                AS boardings,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (
        WHERE f.fare_class_id LIKE 'HS%'
           OR f.fare_class_id LIKE 'SV%'
    )                                                       AS student_boardings,
    COUNT(DISTINCT f.ticket_id)                             AS unique_tickets,
    SUM(f.amount)                                           AS revenue
FROM fact_boardings AS f
JOIN dim_date       AS d
  ON f.date_key = d.date_key
GROUP BY f.route_id, f.hour, d.day_type;
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent This aggregate is the data contract behind the hourly demand curve, the route-by-hour heatmap, and the student-share decomposition used in the demand and school-aware themes.

\section*{A.7 Exploratory Findings in Detail}
\label{appendix:eda}

This appendix expands the five exploratory views of the 90-day AFC
window whose headline numbers are summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:solution:dataset:eda}.

\noindent\textbf{Daily ridership over the 90-day window.} The daily
boarding total traces the overall demand trajectory across the AFC
window. A mid-February trough is visible that aligns with the
Tết public holiday, when much of the city's working and student
population leaves for family travel and the bus network's regular commuter
load drops correspondingly.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eda_daily_boardings.png}
\caption{Daily AFC boardings, 1 January to 31 March 2026}
\label{fig:eda:daily}
\end{figure}

Daily counts oscillate around a weekly cycle for the first three weeks
of January at a median of approximately 228{,}000 boardings per day, then
decline ahead of the Tết public holiday and reach a single-day
minimum on 16 February at 33{,}176 boardings (an 84 percent drop from
the January baseline). Ridership recovers progressively through the
second half of February and returns to the January baseline by the
first week of March.

\noindent\textbf{Hourly demand by day type.} Disaggregating the daily
totals by hour and day type reveals the commuter signature of the
network's weekday demand and the contrasting flatter shape of weekend
demand.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{eda_hourly_by_daytype.png}
\caption{Average hourly boardings, weekday vs weekend}
\label{fig:eda:hourly}
\end{figure}

Weekday demand has two roughly equal peaks (about 22{,}000 events per hour
at approximately 6 AM and 4 to 5 PM); weekend demand is flatter, lower in
absolute magnitude, and shifted to later in the day with no comparable
morning peak.

\noindent\textbf{Fare-class composition by hour.} Decomposing the hourly
totals by fare class shows which sub-population drives each part of the
day.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{eda_fareclass_by_hour.png}
\caption{Hourly boarding composition by fare-class group}
\label{fig:eda:fareclass}
\end{figure}

Across the day, regular cash tickets dominate by volume
(approximately 43 percent of all boardings) but the student fare group
(approximately 36 percent, the HS-SV discounted class) drives a
disproportionate share of the morning peak. Free or subsidized tickets
contribute approximately 16 percent and are roughly flat across the
day; the small remainder is split between paper and other ticket types
(approximately 6 percent combined).

\noindent\textbf{Route volume distribution.} The boarding count per route
is heavy-tailed, with a small number of routes carrying a disproportionate
share of the total. Figure~\ref{fig:eda:pareto} ranks the top 50 routes
and overlays the cumulative share.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eda_route_pareto.png}
\caption{Top 50 routes by boarding volume, with cumulative share}
\label{fig:eda:pareto}
\end{figure}

The boarding distribution is heavy-tailed. Cumulative share reaches 80
percent at the 55th-ranked route of 128 active routes; the top 10 routes
account for roughly 32 percent of all boardings. The pattern motivates the
top-$k$ ranked-bar visualizations chosen in
Section~\ref{sec:solution:viz} and the operator-scorecard view in the
Financial and Operations theme.

\noindent\textbf{Payment-channel trend.} Stacking the daily volume by
payment channel reveals whether the cash dominance reported in earlier
statistics is uniform across the window or whether it shifts over time.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eda_payment_trend.png}
\caption{Daily boardings stacked by payment channel}
\label{fig:eda:payment}
\end{figure}

Cash payment dominates throughout the window (approximately 96.2 percent
of daily volume), with a small and roughly constant non-cash band
underneath. The Tết-period dip is visible on both channels in
proportion, confirming that the dip is a network-wide phenomenon rather
than a channel-specific effect.
