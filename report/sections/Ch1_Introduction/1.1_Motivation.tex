\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:intro:motivation}

The Ho Chi Minh City public bus network is a city-scale public-transit
system. It operates approximately 150 routes served by more than 2,000
vehicles and is managed by the Center for Public Transport Management.
The network produces two operational data streams continuously: Global
Positioning System (GPS) vehicle telemetry and Automated Fare Collection
(AFC) fare records, together with static reference tables for stops,
routes, and operating companies.

Despite the volume of data the network produces, operational decisions
are still made largely from manual inspection of static reports and
spreadsheet exports. Anomalies that are visible in the data, including
chronically delayed stops, routes with a disproportionate share of
near-empty trips, and buses that miss the connection window at metro
interchange stations, go undetected until they escalate into service
failures. Three user roles depend on the network: the passenger, the
operating company, and the public-transport authority. Each role brings
analytical questions that the existing tools cannot answer.

The project proposed in this report is built on a single design claim:
the city's bus network produces approximately 17.8~million boarding
events every quarter and approximately fifty days of vehicle telemetry
per release window, but no \textit{publicly accessible, multi-stakeholder
analytical surface} over this dataset has been built. The proposed
system fills that gap through a coordinated catalog of twenty
charts answering fourteen analytical questions across the three user
roles, with school-aware demand as its most distinctive contribution.

The publicly accessible tools that exist today cover passenger
navigation and ticketing rather than analytical inspection. Three mobile
applications support journey planning, a fourth bundles multi-modal
routing with the city's electronic-ticketing card, an operator-specific
application covers the electric-bus subnetwork, and one web directory
lists the routes and their stops. A single research demonstrator
publishes one spatial-density view. None of these artefacts provides any
aggregate, comparative, temporal, or financial analysis across the
network. Internal control-room dashboards may exist inside the operating
authority, but they are not exposed to operators, passengers, or
researchers; the absence of an externally accessible analytical surface
is the gap that this project addresses.

The opening of Metro Line 1 in December 2024 has sharpened the gap by
introducing an intermodal dependency. The metro's effectiveness
depends on bus feeders arriving on time at interchange stations; a bus
arriving five minutes late at an interchange translates directly into
a missed metro departure for its passengers. Monitoring this
dependency at scale requires integrating the bus operational data with
the metro timetable, a capability that the existing operational tools
cannot provide.
